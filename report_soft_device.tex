\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We implement the $h$-model of Chaintron, Caruel and Kimmig (Math.\ in Action 12, 2023) with $N$ heads and add a closure for the sliding variable $x_c$ in which the total force is imposed and the sliding speed is an output (soft device). Section~\ref{sec:notation} defines every symbol, and marks those that are not in the paper. The note then states what is imposed and what is measured in each numerical experiment, defines every plotted quantity, and reports the results: the force--speed relation with its S-shaped part when lengthening is allowed, and stick--slip cycles in a mixed device (a spring in series, far end driven). Calibration choices that are inferred rather than read from the paper are listed in Section~\ref{sec:caveats}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

The paper computes the force--velocity curve in \emph{velocity clamp}: the sliding velocity $\dot x_c$ is imposed and the mean force per head is measured (its Fig.~5.2). In a force experiment the roles are reversed. Here we do both, with the same head-level model:
\begin{itemize}
\item velocity clamp gives the steady-state force $\tau_{ss}(u)$ at imposed shortening speed $u=-\dot x_c$;
\item the soft device imposes a load, adds a macroscopic viscosity $\nu$ on $x_c$, and measures $u(t)$;
\item the mixed device adds a spring between $x_c$ and a far end that is held or driven.
\end{itemize}
The first two are linked by a steady-state relation (Section~\ref{sec:steady}) whose non-monotone part produces the S.

\section{The $h$-model as implemented}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{State and energies}
Head $i$ carries $(\alpha_i,X_i,h_i)$, with $h_i=0$ when attached. The energies $w_0,w_1$ are the regularised piecewise quadratic potentials of Table~D.1 (Appendix~\ref{app:params}). A head transmits force to the actin only when attached:
\begin{equation}
f_i=\alpha_i\,w_1'(X_i),\qquad F_{\rm mot}=\sum_{i=1}^Nf_i=N\hat\tau .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Jumps and detailed balance}
Four channels, each with its own clock $c$ and threshold $e\sim\mathcal E(1)$ (Algorithm~1 of the paper):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cll}
\toprule
channel & transition & rate \\
\midrule
A & $0\to1$ (direct attachment) & $K_{0\to1}(x,h)$ \\
B & $0\to1$ (reverse of detachment) & $K^{\rm rev}_{1\to0}(x,h)$ \\
C & $1\to0$ (direct detachment) & $k_{1\to0}(x_{\rm att})=\int K_{1\to0}(x_{\rm att},h)\,\dd h$ \\
D & $1\to0$ (reverse of attachment) & $k^{\rm rev}_{0\to1}(x_{\rm att})=\int K^{\rm rev}_{0\to1}(x_{\rm att},h)\,\dd h$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A and B are active for a detached head, with $x=X_i$ and $h=h_i$. C and D are active for an attached head, with $x_{\rm att}=X_i$. $K_{0\to1}$ and $K^{\rm rev}_{1\to0}$ are the primitive rates of Table~D.1. The other two follow from detailed balance. With the pairing $x_{\rm det}=x_{\rm att}-h$,
\begin{align}
K^{\rm rev}_{0\to1}(x_{\rm att},h)&=\frac1d\,K_{0\to1}(x_{\rm det},h)\,\exp\!\Big[\frac{w_1(x_{\rm att})-w_0(x_{\rm det})}{\kBT}\Big],\\
K_{1\to0}(x_{\rm att},h)&=\frac1d\,K^{\rm rev}_{1\to0}(x_{\rm det},h)\,\exp\!\Big[\frac{w_1(x_{\rm att})-w_0(x_{\rm det})+\mu_T}{\kBT}\Big].
\end{align}
The factor $1/d$ is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:caveats}. On detachment through C or D, the landing value $h'$ is drawn from the density $K_{1\to0}(x_{\rm att},\cdot)$ or $K^{\rm rev}_{0\to1}(x_{\rm att},\cdot)$ using tabulated cumulative distributions.

\subsection{Time stepping}
At each step $\delta t=0.002$ ms: (i) compute $F_{\rm mot}$ and fix $\Delta x_c$ (Section~\ref{sec:closure}); (ii) update the clocks of the active channels and select the channel with the largest positive residual $c-e$, if any; (iii) move detached tips by Euler--Maruyama, $\Delta X=-\eta^{-1}w_0'(X)\delta t+\sqrt{2\kBT\delta t/\eta}\,\xi$, $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, with $h\leftarrow h+\Delta x_c-\Delta X \pmod d$, and move attached tips by $\Delta x_c$; (iv) apply jumps: attachment sets $X\leftarrow X+h+\Delta x_c$, $h\leftarrow0$; detachment sets $X\leftarrow X+\Delta x_c-h'$, $h\leftarrow h'$; (v) reset the clocks of jumping heads. Numerically, $K_{1\to0}$ and $K^{\rm rev}_{0\to1}$ are tabulated on a grid of $x_{\rm att}\in[-100,100]$ nm (step $0.2$) and $h$ (midpoints, step $0.25$ nm); exponents are clipped to $\pm30$ and tips to $\pm100$ nm.

\section{Imposing the sliding}
\label{sec:closure}

\subsection{Velocity clamp (the paper's protocol)}
$\dot x_c=v$ is prescribed. Heads are then independent, and an ensemble of $N$ heads with random initial $h_i$ is a Monte Carlo sample of one head. We measure $\langle\hat\tau\rangle$ and $\langle n_a\rangle$, averaged over the $N$ heads and over time after a burn-in, and define
\begin{equation}
\tau_{ss}(u):=\langle\hat\tau\rangle\big|_{\dot x_c=-u}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Soft device}
The actin is rigid with coordinate $x_c$, subject to the drag $-\nu\dot x_c$, the external force and the motor force. All $x_c$-dependence of the energy $E=\sum_i\alpha_iw_1(X_i)$ comes from the attached heads ($\partial X_i/\partial x_c=1$), so $\partial E/\partial x_c=F_{\rm mot}$. Overdamped force balance gives
\begin{equation}
F_{\rm ext}+F_{\rm mot}+\nu\,\dot x_c=0,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\nu\,\dot x_c=-F_{\rm ext}-\sum_{i=1}^N f_i .
\label{eq:closure}
\end{equation}
Every term is a force in the direction of contraction: $F_{\rm ext}>0$ assists contraction, $F_{\rm mot}>0$ is traction, and the drag on a filament moving at shortening speed $u$ is $-\nu u=+\nu\dot x_c$. Equation \eqref{eq:closure} is the same update as the $\delta s$ step of the earlier $N$-head JAP codes, with $v\leftrightarrow\nu$ and $F\leftrightarrow-F_{\rm ext}$. If the opposite sign convention is used (force $F^{(+x)}_{\rm mot}=-\sum_if_i$ along $+x$), the balance reads $F^{(+x)}_{\rm ext}+F^{(+x)}_{\rm mot}-\nu\dot x_c=0$ and is the same equation.

\paragraph{Per-head form.} With $\tilde\nu=\nu/N$, $F_{\rm ext}=-N\bar p$ and $F_{\rm mot}=N\hat\tau$,
\begin{equation}
\hat\tau=\bar p+\tilde\nu\,u .
\label{eq:perhead}
\end{equation}
The force on the filament is $N\hat\tau$, not $\hat\tau$.

\paragraph{Numerics.} At fixed $\alpha$, $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}F_{\rm mot}=K_+\dot x_c$ (for the clipped stiffness), which makes \eqref{eq:closure} stiff with rate $K_+/\nu$. We use the semi-implicit step
\begin{equation}
\Delta x_c=-\frac{(F_{\rm ext}+F_{\rm mot})\,\delta t}{\nu+K_+\,\delta t}.
\end{equation}
There is no thermal noise on $x_c$.

\subsection{Mixed device}
A spring of stiffness $Nk_L$ links the actin to a far end at $x_L(t)=x_{L,0}+Vt$, giving the load per head $\bar p=k_L(x_L-x_c)=\bar p_0+k_LVt-k_Lx_c$. This is a spring in series with the cluster, like the elastic backbone $k_b$ of Fig.~7(b) of Caruel, Allain and Truskinovsky (J.\ Mech.\ Phys.\ Solids 76, 2015). In that figure the force $T$ is applied at the two ends, whereas here the far end is held or driven in position. The closure \eqref{eq:perhead} is unchanged, with $F_{\rm ext}=-N\bar p(t,x_c)$ now depending on $x_c$. $V=0$ is a fixed far end.

\section{Steady states under force control and the S}
\label{sec:steady}

\subsection{Steady-state relation}
In a steady state at speed $u$ the mean force per head is $\tau_{ss}(u)$, so \eqref{eq:perhead} gives the load that sustains that speed:
\begin{equation}
\pss(u)=\tau_{ss}(u)-\tilde\nu\,u .
\label{eq:pbar}
\end{equation}
For $\tilde\nu\to0$ this is the paper's curve, which corresponds to the experimental force-clamp protocol. A positive $\tilde\nu$ shifts each point horizontally by $-\tilde\nu u$.

\subsection{Stability (heuristic)}
Assume the head population relaxes to $\tau_{ss}$ with a single time $\theta$, $\theta\dot{\hat\tau}=\tau_{ss}(u)-\hat\tau$, with $u=(\hat\tau-\bar p)/\tilde\nu$ from \eqref{eq:perhead}. Linearising,
\begin{equation}
\theta\,\delta\dot{\hat\tau}=\Big(\frac{\tau_{ss}'(u)}{\tilde\nu}-1\Big)\delta\hat\tau,
\end{equation}
so a steady state is stable if and only if $\tau_{ss}'(u)<\tilde\nu$, equivalently $\pss'(u)<0$. This is a one-timescale argument, a criterion for the sign and not for the location.

\subsection{Origin of the fold}
Where $\tau_{ss}$ is non-monotone, $\pss(u)$ is multivalued as a function of the load. We measured $\tau_{ss}$ decreasing monotonically for lengthening ($u<0$), and a slight rise for small $u>0$ (from $0.88$ to $0.94$ pN, comparable to the scatter), as the paper also notes for small shortening speeds. For $\tilde\nu=0.05$ this leaves a stable shortening branch ending at $\pss\simeq1.03\,\tauz$ ($u\simeq0.2$), an unstable stretch around the stall point, and a fast-lengthening branch $u\simeq(\hat\tau-\bar p)/\tilde\nu$ whose minimum load is $\simeq0.59\,\tauz$ at $|u|\simeq3$. Increasing $\tilde\nu$ removes the fold (Table~\ref{tab:fold}).

\section{Results}

\subsection{Validation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Thermodynamic consistency.} At $\mu_T=0$ with $\dot x_c=0$ the net cycling vanishes: $(n_A-n_D)/(n_A+n_D)=0.002$ and $(n_C-n_B)/(n_C+n_B)=-0.003$ ($N=600$, 90 ms). At $\mu_T=100$ zJ the net flux is positive.
\item \emph{Isometric indicators} (Table~5.1: $\check n_a=0.151$, $\check\tau_c=6.39$ pN). We obtain $n_a\simeq0.14$--$0.15$ and $\check\tau_c\simeq5$--$6$ pN, depending on the seed. The spread reflects the quenched $h$ distribution.
\item \emph{Force--velocity curve (Fig.~5.2).} $\tau_{ss}/\tauz\simeq0.29$ at $u=5\umps$ and $\simeq0.06$ at $15\umps$, which agrees with the curve as read from the figure.
\item \emph{Closure.} In the soft-device runs $\bar p+\tilde\nu u=\hat\tau$ holds to within 0.003 pN when each term is averaged over a 40 ms window.
\end{itemize}

The upward ramp follows the shortening branch, stalls ($u\approx0$) close to $\tauz$, and then yields to lengthening at speeds up to $\simeq21\umps$ ($\simeq(\hat\tau-\bar p)/\tilde\nu$, with few heads attached). \subsection{Dependence on $N$}
Equation \eqref{eq:pbar} is the $N\to\infty$ limit. At finite $N$, $u=(\hat\tau-\bar p)/\tilde\nu$ inherits the fluctuations of $\hat\tau$, of order $\sigma_f/\sqrt N$ (a Bernoulli estimate gives $\sigma_f\approx\sqrt{n_a(1-n_a)}\,\check\tau_c\approx2$ pN). The fluctuation of the speed is then $\delta u\sim\sigma_f/(\tilde\nu\sqrt N)$, about $4\umps$ for $N=100$ and $\tilde\nu=0.05$ (an order-of-magnitude estimate), comparable to the mean speed. This is consistent with what we observe: at $N=100$, $\tilde\nu=0.05$ and $\bar p=0.28$ pN the mean speed is $2.45\umps$ with $\hat\tau=0.41$ pN and $n_a=0.14$, whereas the clamp curve gives $\tau_{ss}(2.45)\simeq0.58$ pN at the same $n_a$. The mean force per attached head is lower in the soft device because heads experience a fluctuating speed.

Two trends are supported by the data: the recapture load moves toward the predicted floor as $N$ grows ($0.74\to0.59$), and the $N=30$ system does not stay on either branch. The yield loads ($1.15,1.14,0.85$) are not monotone in $N$ and come from single runs, so no trend is claimed for them.

With a fixed far end no sustained oscillation was obtained. A two-variable heuristic for a stall point with elastic load $k_L$ (my own, untested against the simulation) uses $\theta$, $\tilde K$ and $\tau_{ss}'(0)$ as defined in Section~\ref{sec:notation}. It gives $\det=k_L/(\tilde\nu\theta)>0$ and a Hopf instability when
\begin{equation}
\tau_{ss}'(0)>\theta\,(\tilde K+k_L)+\tilde\nu .
\end{equation}
For a constant load ($k_L=0$) $\det=0$ and the heuristic excludes a Hopf bifurcation. A length-step test to measure $\theta$ and $\tilde K$ was inconclusive (the force rose after a release, which I traced to the structure of $w_1$ in Section~\ref{sec:caveats}), so the condition has not been evaluated.

\subsection{Driven far end: stick--slip in the mixed device}
Now $V>0$: the far end is stretched at speed $V$, and $\bar p(t)=\bar p_0+k_LVt-k_Lx_c$.

\paragraph{Mechanism.} In steady lengthening at speed $V$ ($u=-V$), \eqref{eq:pbar} gives the sustaining load $\pss(-V)=\tau_{ss}(-V)+\tilde\nu V$. Our measured $\tau_{ss}$ decreases with lengthening speed (Table~\ref{tab:clamp}), so for $\tilde\nu=0.05$ this load decreases with $V$ for roughly $0.3\lesssim V\lesssim6\umps$. Suppose the speed follows the branch $\pss(u)=k_Lg$ quasi-statically, with $g=x_L-x_c$. Then $\dot g=V+u$ and $\delta u=(k_L/\pss'(u))\,\delta g$, so
\begin{equation}
\delta\dot g=\frac{k_L}{\pss'(u)}\,\delta g,
\end{equation}
which grows when $\pss'(u)>0$. This is the same unstable condition as in Section~\ref{sec:steady}, and it holds for any $k_L>0$. The stable parts are the stalled state and the fast-lengthening branch, so the expected motion is stick--slip: the load rises while the heads hold ($u\approx0$), the filament slips, and the load falls until the heads recapture. The argument ignores the head kinetics.

\paragraph{Runs.} $\tilde\nu=0.05$, $\bar p_0=0.50$ pN, $t_{\max}=200$ ms, one realisation each. The load is computed from $x_c(t)$. A ``peak'' is a maximum of the 1 ms-smoothed $\bar p$ above $1.0\,\tauz$ separated from the previous peak by more than 5 ms; the FFT is of $\bar p$ after 40 ms.

\section{Caveats and limitations}
\label{sec:caveats}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Factor $1/d$ in the detailed-balance rates.} Taken literally, (3.4)--(3.5) of the paper give $n_a=0.03$. Since $K_{0\to1}$ and $K^{\rm rev}_{1\to0}$ are rates while $K_{1\to0}$ and $K^{\rm rev}_{0\to1}$ are densities in $h$, the relation needs a length, and for the uniform measure on the torus it is $d$ (parameter \texttt{H\_NORM}). With it, $n_a$ matches Table~5.1. The $\mu_T=0$ test passes for any value of this factor, so it does not validate it.
\item \textbf{Potentials.} Evaluated from Table~D.1 as extracted from the PDF text, $w_1$ and $w_0$ agree with Fig.~5.1(a) in the difference $w_0-w_1$ at the minima ($\approx77$ zJ) but differ by a constant of about 30 zJ in absolute value. A constant cancels in all rates. I did not compare the curves point by point. \emph{In addition}, inside the regularisation window $[x_\ell,x_r]=[1.3,2.5]$ nm the transcribed interpolation (Hermite terms plus the bump $\check w\,\varphi_5$ with $\check w=5.7$ zJ) gives $w_1'$ between $-9$ and $+23$ pN and $w_1''$ between $-71$ and $+49$ pN/nm. Attached heads sit at $x_{\rm att}\simeq2.2\pm3.2$ nm, so this window is populated. I could not check against Fig.~5.1(a) whether the paper's potential has this structure. All force-dependent results here (stall force, yield and recapture loads, the elastic response) are for the potential as transcribed.
\item \textbf{Table D.1 reading.} The box in $x$ of $K_{0\to1}$ is taken as $[x^+_\ell,x^+_r]=[1.5,17]$ nm (a positive interval, as in the text of Section~5.1); the exponent in $K^{\rm rev}_{1\to0}$ is clipped to $\pm30$ for numerical safety. The paper's text and Table~D.1 are not fully consistent about which of $K_{1\to0}$, $K^{\rm rev}_{1\to0}$ is the primitive rate; I took the table at face value.
\item \textbf{Isometric normalisation.} At $\dot x_c=0$ the ensemble is quenched in $h$ (a head whose site is more than about 4 nm away rarely attaches), so $\langle\hat\tau\rangle$ at $v=0$ depends on the sample (0.66 pN against 0.88--0.94 pN at $|u|=0.1$). We therefore define $\tauz$ from $u=\pm0.1$.
\item \textbf{Statistics.} One run per velocity and per $(N,\tilde\nu)$ in the ramps; no repeated runs, no confidence intervals, and no time-step convergence study (all runs use $\delta t=0.002$ ms).
\item \textbf{Free parameters.} $\tilde\nu$, $k_L$, $V$ and $\bar p_0$ are not given by the paper; the values used are my choices.
\item \textbf{No noise on $x_c$.} The closure is deterministic, so at finite $N$ and $\mu_T=0$ the stationary law is not the soft-ensemble Gibbs measure.
\item \textbf{Heuristics.} The stability criterion of Section~\ref{sec:steady}, the stick--slip argument and the Hopf condition use quasi-static or single-relaxation-time assumptions, and the $\delta u$ estimate is of order of magnitude only.
\item \textbf{Range.} The clamp sweep reaches $u=-6\umps$ on the lengthening side, whereas the ramps explore lengthening up to $-21\umps$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Rate formulas}
Let $b(h;\lambda,a)=\tfrac12[1+\tanh\lambda(h+a)]$ for $h<0$ and $\tfrac12[1-\tanh\lambda(h-a)]$ for $h\ge0$. The primitive rates are
\begin{align}
K_{0\to1}(x,h)&=k_+\,b(h;\lambda_{h,+},h_+)\,\chi(x),\\
\chi(x)&=\begin{cases}\tfrac12[1+\tanh\lambda_{x,+}(x-x_{\ell,+})],&x<\tfrac12(x_{\ell,+}+x_{r,+}),\\[2pt]\tfrac12[1-\tanh\lambda_{x,+}(x-x_{r,+})],&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\\
K^{\rm rev}_{1\to0}(x,h)&=k_m\,\tfrac12[1-\tanh\lambda_{x,m}(x-x_m)]\,b(h;\lambda_{h,m},h_m)\nonumber\\
&\quad+\Big\{k_p\,\tfrac12[1-\tanh\lambda_{x,p}(x-x_p)]\,b(h;\lambda_{h,p},h_p)+k_b\,b(h;\lambda_{h,b},h_b)\Big\}\nonumber\\
&\qquad\times e^{[w_0(x)-w_1(x+h)-\mu_T]/\kBT}.
\end{align}
